## Wake routing

Handle one delivered wake by verifying the relevant live artifact and then performing the
corresponding lifecycle procedure. An architect-addressed wake reaches the architect that owns its
issue: the issue's own architect while it holds its role (the daemon starts a child's with the
child's first phase), else the nearest architect above that holds its role, then the root's. A
`catch-up` reaches only the architect of its own issue. A `child-status` or `child-closed` of a
child, and a `worker-died` or `worker-stalled` about a child's own architect, reach the architect
above the child. `phase-finished`, worker lifecycle, child lifecycle, and design-gate wakes are
architect-only and never go to an active phase worker. Where a row below tells you to ask a
person, a child's own architect sends the question to the architect above it instead
(`skill://legion-architect`, "Every turn").

A wake of a `low` kind is read at your next turn: it wakes no released pod and starts no turn, since its act can wait.
The `low` kinds are the rows below that say so:

- A `phase-finished` that the daemon has already moved on from: an implementer's, a tester's or a merger's, a reviewer's request for changes, the design gate's opening, or your own move back out of `accepting`.
  A planner's `phase-finished` and a reviewer's approval are `med`.
- A `held` that records your own escalation (`hold: escalation`).
- A `child-status` with `change: todo`, `change: joined` or `change: started`.
- A `checks-red` notice while a worker holds the phase the issue stays in.
- A `pr-merged` notice.
- A `ci-settled` message while a worker holds the phase.

A Dispatch event that the Legion daemon's own session wrote comes at `low` as well, and its card waits for your next turn.
An `ask.*`, an `issue.released` and an `issue.closed` event are not.
Every other wake is `med` and starts a turn.

A "section N" below is that section of `skill://legion-architect`. The spec and gate steps of
section 1 are in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`; the detail of sections 3, 6
and 7 (a conflict, the hand-over, the close) is in
`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`.

| Wake | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| `carried-gate-opened` | Only a root whose catch-up said `carried` gets it. Its `version`, the person who acted (`actor`) and its `ref`, the old root's document: a human approved that document at `version`, which opened the gate this root carried; the daemon has started any child waiting on it. It names no step: the carry brings the old root's gate records, and the catch-up reports every gated step the carry did not bring as unregistered, so register each of those on this issue's own document for that step (`skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`). It is not `design-approved`: do not proceed to section 2 or release a wave because of it, and start nothing. |
| `catch-up` | The daemon sends it at each launch of your session, once that session is ready, to the architect of its own issue alone. A root's catch-up names its tree: the generation, each design-gate step (`gates`), every issue of the tree that is not done with its phase and status (`issues`), each done issue by its key alone (`done`), and each child you filed and have not released (`unreleased`). A child architect's catch-up names its own issue and every issue under it the same way, and no gate: a child registers none. Either one names in its `accepting`, as waiting for your acceptance, only the issues in `accepting` whose acceptance is yours. Accept each one as section 6 says, a parent only after its last round ("The last round" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). Otherwise, start or resume your work from it as your role prompt says. |
| `child-status` | A child of your tree left the workflow or re-entered it, or joined your tree from the admission queue or from a closed tree. The notice names the child (`issue`) and its `change`: `left`, with the status it left with (`status`); `todo`; `joined`, with `from_tree` naming the old root of the closed tree your release took it from, and no `from_tree` for the admission queue; `started`; `beneath-accepted`; or `reparented`. `change: started` comes at the child's first phase, and the child's own architect starts with it. A `child-status` with `change: todo`, `joined` or `started` is `low`: read it at your next turn, since you start nothing for it. From then on that architect owns the child while it holds its role: its decomposition, its children and its acceptance. While that architect holds its role, you hear only the child's `child-status` and `child-closed`, and its architect's `worker-died` and `worker-stalled`. The child's other notices come to you too while that architect does not hold its role, which the daemon's `holdsRole` decides. `change: todo` (a human's move, your `release_children`, or your `rerun_child`) means it runs again under your tree from planning, and the daemon starts it; you start nothing. A child held for a blocker starts the same way once nothing holds it, with `change: started`: its `waited_on` names the issues it waited on, now done, and a start with no `waited_on` is a child that reached the front of the waiting line, which covers a person or you taking its blocked-by links off; you start nothing for it either. `change: left` with `backlog`, `icebox` or `triage` (a human's move, or your `park_child`) means the child stopped as workflow's `leave` decides, and it advances no further until it is set back to `todo`. The child's next start resumes its architect's session, and that architect starts in a new session only once supervise's `forgetSession` has dropped that one. A `park_child` and then a `rerun_child` is that child's restart and not a park, because the daemon refuses `rerun_child` on a child whose phase is not `done` (`CHILD_RUNNING`, `packages/daemon/internal/api/architect.go`). Joined from the admission queue means a root that waited for a slot and never ran, which a person made a child of your tree or your `release_children` adopted, now runs in your tree as a child, from planning when it is `todo` and parked otherwise. It takes an admission slot of its own when its work starts, and its own architect starts with its first phase. One that waits on an unfinished blocked-by issue is held for that blocker like any other child, and starts when nothing holds it. You start nothing. `change: beneath-accepted`, with the ancestor in `accepted_ancestor`, is a person's `todo` beneath an ancestor whose reviewed head the daemon has already accepted, which the daemon recorded and holds at `admitted`: nothing starts it until that ancestor closes, and a `request_backward_move` of the ancestor is what runs it under the ancestor now. `change: reparented` names a root that ran (`child`) and that a person made a child of an issue of your tree (`parent`) in Dispatch: it stays a tree of its own (`stays_own` says why), your tree cannot take it in, and its work reaches your tree only through your tree's own issues. What the other issues you own do is your decision. When a park leaves no child that an issue in `accepting` waits on, run that issue's last round (`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). |
| `child-closed` | Read the child completion and remaining open children. Re-scope open work, and take out with `park_child` each child the result makes unneeded (first take its link off each sibling that waits on it, or park that sibling too when its work ends with the parked child, under the same rule, as section 2 says) and say why with `dispatch comment`. Park a child only when a person asked for that move, or when the work is done, folded into another issue or no longer needed. Then file any new child the result calls for, after the red-team check in `skill://legion-architect/references/decomposition.md`, and release it at once with `release_children`, blocked by what it builds on (section 2). When a worker waits on this child for a missing surface (see `phase-finished`), tell it to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic. The last child's close is not the end-game (section 3). When it was the last child an issue in `accepting` waits on, run that issue's last round (`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). |
| `child-release-stalled` | It names a child that a `release_children` released and your tree has not recorded (`child`), its parent (`parent`), and why (`stall`), one of these. `attempts-spent`: the parent's release has not reached the daemon after the tries `attempts` counts, so inspect the tree before you try again. `unrecorded-ancestor` or `unrecorded-blocker`: GitHub did not answer the App installation read, or the default-branch read past GitHub's rate limit, of a repository on the child's parent chain (`repo`), and the release left the child unrecorded because a parent on that chain is not `todo` (`ancestor`), or did not write the child `todo` because its parent is not recorded (`blocker`, with its `blocker_status`). `recovered-unrecorded`: the daemon's recovery of such repositories (`repos`), once GitHub answered, still left children unrecorded, each named in `unrecorded` with its parent and whether that parent is recorded (`parent_recorded`). `let-go-no-todo` or `let-go-answered`: the daemon no longer waits for GitHub to answer for such repositories (`repos`), because no `todo` issue labelled for Legion or under a parent names one now (`let-go-no-todo`), or because Dispatch's repository mapping, or a read GitHub answered at another issue's preparation, answers for it now (`let-go-answered`). No recovery records the children that a release left to it, each named in `let_go` with its parent, whether that parent is recorded (`parent_recorded`), its Dispatch status (`status`, empty where Dispatch no longer lists it), its repository (`repo`) and the refusal of that repository (`refusal`), if any. Where `for_now` is true, the daemon records the child once GitHub answers for its repository and the child is `todo`. Where `refusal` is set and `for_now` is false, install the App the refusal names on the repository, or set a repository both Legion Apps are installed on, then release the child again. Otherwise, release the child again once it is `todo`. The first two come to the architect whose `release_children` it was. The recovery's notice and the let-go notices go, whoever ran the release, to the architect that owns each child's parent where your tree records that parent, and to the tree root's architect for the rest: a notice on each recorded parent lists that parent's children, and one on the tree root lists the rest, each with its parent. Read the parent's record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` (`issue` the parent); release the parent first where it is not recorded, then run `release_children` for the child again once the parent is recorded in your tree, and tell the controller if it never is. |
| `child-not-recorded` | It names a child your tree did not record (`child`), the repository it resolved to (`resolved`) and the daemon's refusal (`refusal`): its repository resolves to none (neither it nor an ancestor names one, and the project has no default), or a role GitHub App is not installed on that repository, which the refusal names. The daemon said why on the child too. Set the child's repository (`dispatch issue-update --repo owner/name`), or get the named App installed on the repository it has, then release it again with `release_children`. Tell the controller when neither is yours to do. |
| `children-first` | An issue of your tree at `admitted` starts no phase of its own while Dispatch shows an open child under it, a child you filed and have not released included. The notice names the parent (`issue`) and each open child (`children`) with the Dispatch status that holds it (`status`), whether you released it (`released`), and its cycle (`cycle`): the issue above it on its own parent chain that a `blocked_by` link of that child names, which only a person taking that link off ends, so open a `dispatch ask`. It comes once per issue and run of its tree. Release every child that should run. Park a child only when a person asked for that move, or when the work is done, folded into another issue or no longer needed, and cite that reason in a comment on the child. A released child, a held one included, stays under the parent, because the parent's wait ends when that child leaves the workflow. Move out from under the parent only a child you have not released and that should not run now. Move it under another issue you own (section 3 says which) that has started a phase and has not reached `accepting`, and never under an issue it waits on: that move ends this wait and leaves the child in the tree (section 3). After the move, ask the person on the root (`dispatch ask`) when that child's work should run or stop: release it when they say to run it; when they say to stop it, close it, or move it to `icebox` under the rule when they say its work should wait, citing their answer. Until they answer, sign off neither the issue you moved it under nor any issue above it that you accept. Never give such a child the `legion` label, and never clear its parent while the tree is live, because such a child would then run nowhere (section 3). Where you own no issue to move the child under, ask the person. A child's architect sends each of these questions to the architect above it, as "Every turn" in `skill://legion-architect` says. The parent's first phase can start once the last open child has left the workflow; a parent the daemon has already started gives its admission slot back while it waits for its children, and takes one again by itself; nothing of its work stops meanwhile, and you do nothing for it (https://cybermonklabs.github.io/legion/legion/concepts/#admission-and-the-legion-label, which also names the three roots that keep their slot through a wait); a parent the daemon has not started waits in the line for a slot. You start nothing. |
| `checks-red` | The daemon asked the implementer to merge the base forward onto a head that conflicts with it, and the issue stays in the phase the notice names (`phase`: `testing`, `reviewing`, `accepting`, `retro` or `merging`). It names the head (`head`), the base (`conflicts_with`, absent where the daemon has not recorded the pull request's base), the pull request (`pull_request`) and why the daemon acted (`why`). It is `low` while a worker holds that phase, since that worker is told to hold and the implementer is asked: read it at your next turn. It is `med` in `accepting`: do not `sign_off` the conflicting head, and wait for the reviewer's approval of the new head once the merge lands. You start nothing for the merge. It counts no round. |
| `ci-settled` | A CI settlement on the pull request's head: each check that did not pass with its conclusion (`not_passed`), each commit status in `failure` or `error` with its state (`not_passed_statuses`), the count of the checks and statuses that passed (`passed`) and the `gh pr checks` command that lists them (`list_passed`). It moves nothing. It is `low` while a worker holds the issue's phase, since that worker is told too: read it at your next turn. It is `med` where no worker holds the phase (`admitted`, `accepting`, `held`, `awaiting_person`). A red there is yours to decide on, as your role prompt says, and the worker that holds the phase re-runs a red that failed on itself. |
| `design-approved` | Its `version` is the approved one, and it names no step: under more than one gated step, read `gatedSteps` in your own record (the `legion` tool's `read_record`) to see which step that version opened and whether another still holds the tree. A human approved the document of one gated step at its current version; that step is open. Proceed to section 2 once every gated step is `approved` — except on a root whose catch-up said `handed_over`: its workers already run their phases, so start nothing. One whose `version` is the version a human's own answer to a decision block wrote, after an earlier approval (Dispatch carried that approval to it, "After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), opens nothing new: carry on, and release no wave because of it. A root that was waiting on a question in `awaiting_person` gets no such notice: the task that resumes it carries `gate_open: true` and the sentence "this resume is your design-approved wake", and that resume is this wake — proceed to section 2 on it. |
| `design-changes-requested` | Its `version`, and the person's reason in its `reason` block, and no step: under more than one gated step, read `gatedSteps` in your own record (the `legion` tool's `read_record`) and take the step whose `status` is `changes_requested`. A human asked for changes to that step's document at `version`, for that reason. Revise that document as the reason asks and request approval again as "The armed gate" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md` says; end your turn; that step is closed. On a root that was waiting on a question in `awaiting_person`, a review the human gave after the wait began comes in the resume task, not in this notice, whether it answered the wait or came after something else did: the task names the review with its version and reason, unless a later approval superseded it. A `version` below the latest, or a second request for one version, is a request that reached the daemon late: read it as the same section's last paragraph says. Each request arrives once. |
| `phase-finished` | The daemon has already moved the issue to its next phase by its fixed table and started that phase's role; you start nothing. The notice names the phase that ended (`phase`), the phase it moved to (`to`) and the handoff (`handoff_phase`, `handoff_version`). It is `low` for the phase of an implementer, a tester or a merger, for a reviewer's request for changes, for the design gate's opening and for your own move back out of `accepting`: read it at your next turn. Read the finishing phase's handoff on the Dispatch issue at that version (`dispatch handoff-read --issue <issue> --phase <word> --version <n>`); if it shows unresolved gaps, tell the role now working the issue (`envoy_publish` to its role topic). A `planner` notice that names a departure from the spec's design defers to the full condition in "After approval" of `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`: when the approved Summary, the requirements, the checks, the scope and the settled decisions still hold, the plan is the record; otherwise change the root spec and request approval again as that section says. On a child, read the same condition against the child's spec at the version its parent's review approved. When it does not hold, write a new version of the child's spec and have the architect above review it (`skill://legion-architect/references/decomposition.md`, "Reviewing a child's spec"). A `reviewer` notice whose GitHub review is `CHANGES_REQUESTED` needs nothing from you: the daemon has returned the issue to `implementing` (Dispatch `in_progress`) and started the **implementer**, whose correction goes through the tester and the reviewer again, never straight to retro. A `reviewer` notice with an `APPROVED` review means the issue is in `accepting` and the acceptance is yours (section 6): read the evidence, then `sign_off` (a parent only after its last round, "The last round" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`) or send it back with `request_backward_move`. A `tester` notice with `verdict: "fail"` — its handoff carries `implementerProof.verdict: "rejected"`, or a failure naming a check with no pass standing at the head — has gone back to the **implementer** by the daemon's table; never record that check's result from another role. A worker that reports no surface reaches the changed path sends that report instead of completing its phase, so the daemon starts nothing more on that issue and the worker stays idle in its session, not suspended: build the surface in this tree: a child issue (infrastructure, tooling, or a skill) after the red-team check in `skill://legion-architect/references/decomposition.md`, or, when the red team folds it, tell the waiting worker to move the issue back to `implementing` with `request_backward_move`, naming the surface. When it is a child, tell the waiting worker to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic once that child's `child-closed` arrives. That report is never a reason to advance the phase. |
| `pr-blocked` | It names the cap (`cap`), the rounds counted (`counted`) and the conflict rounds not counted (`conflict_rounds`), the implementer's round count: every move back to an earlier phase counts one, and a move made while the head conflicts with its base counts as a conflict round the cap leaves out; the notice comes once for each value the cap holds, so the rounds past the cap tell you nothing again, a cap raised in `legion.yaml` tells again at its new number, a lowered one tells once on the next counted round, and a new generation tells again from its own first crossing. It moves nothing, since the round that reached the cap has already started its worker. Tell the rounds apart by what announced each: a tester's fail by its `phase-finished` with `verdict: "fail"`; a request for changes by the reviewer's `phase-finished` over a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` review; a worker's `request_backward_move` by a `phase-finished` from the role that asked, whose reason reaches only the task of the worker the move started, so ask that worker; your own send-back from `accepting` by the reason you gave. A `checks-red` notice announces no round: the daemon kept the issue in its phase and asked the implementer to merge the base forward. Its `counted` has already left the conflict rounds out, so judge the cap by it. Conflict rounds need nothing from you beyond the next round. Tester fails and requests for changes are the rounds the cap is for; a red check moves nothing and is told to nobody as a notice, so it is no round at all. Each `review_round_cap` notice is a decision you make in the turn it wakes, never one left for the next round to make: read what every counted round found, choose one of the three below, act on it in the same turn, and post it as a `dispatch comment` on the issue naming the rounds you read and the choice. **Narrow**: the change itself is sound but each round finds more around it; tell the implementer and the reviewer (`envoy_publish` to each one's role topic) which checks the next round answers, dropping no requirement; a correctness finding is still fixed in this pull request, and so is a wording nit or any clear fix in a file it touches, so the `Fast-follow:` line carries only cleanup in files it does not touch. **Split**: part of the work cannot settle in this pull request and is a separately deliverable change (`skill://legion-architect`, section 1). On a child, that part becomes a child of this issue's parent, never of this one (this issue closes at its own merge, and the split part usually builds on this pull request's unmerged code). The architect that owns the parent files it. When you are this child's own architect, send the part to the architect above you with `envoy_publish`, since `release_children` refuses an issue outside your own (`ISSUE_NOT_OWNED`). The parent's architect runs the red-team check in `skill://legion-architect/references/decomposition.md`, then files the part: `dispatch issue --project <project> --parent <parent> --title '<outcome>' --blocked-by <this issue> --spec-file -`. It releases the part now with `release_children`: Legion holds it on that `--blocked-by` link until this issue closes, and starts it then. A requirement moves only to that released child. On the root, open a `dispatch ask` for the part that cannot settle here (`skill://legion-worker/references/findings.md`): it stays this issue's requirement, and this pull request carries it, until the person who answers that ask records that the issue will not deliver it. **Accept the rounds**: each round found less than the one before it, and the last found no defect in the change itself (a stale line, a comment that states an old default); let the loop run and say why in the comment. A later notice is a new decision: accept again only when its round found less than the round you last accepted, and otherwise narrow or split. On the root, a finding that changes what the spec requires or what a check proves is a decision block in the root spec ("After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`). On a child, it is a new version of the child's spec, which the architect above the child reviews, and a decision only a person can make goes to the architect above. Neither that spec change nor a `dispatch ask`, which is only for a decision that needs a human's authority, replaces the choice. Judge a worker's backward move by the reason that worker gives. A move that sends back code found wrong counts with the tester fails. |
| `pr-merged` | The pull request merged and the issue did not close; the notice names the pull request (`pull_request`), and its fields say why. It is `low`: read it at your next turn. Merged before the issue reached `merging` (`phase` names the phase it was in): the workflow runs on, and the merge closes the issue the moment it gets there, your acceptance and retro first. Merged while issues beneath it still run: `waiting_for` names every issue the close waits for (those beneath it still in the workflow: on a root, every other issue of its tree), and the issue stays in `merging` with its workers kept, until the last of them is done or parked ("The close" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). Either way you start nothing and no further notice follows. |
| `pr-closed-unmerged` | Decide from current scope whether the work is reopened, started over (for a child, `park_child` and then `rerun_child`, which is that child's restart and not a park, because the daemon refuses `rerun_child` on a child whose phase is not `done`: `CHILD_RUNNING`, `packages/daemon/internal/api/architect.go`), or ended with a reason. A child's own architect cannot park or rerun its own issue (`ISSUE_NOT_OWNED`), so it sends a restart of its issue to the architect above it. Delegate the repository action to the responsible phase worker and keep ownership. |
| `pull-request-missing` | It names the repository (`repo`), the branch (`branch`) and the handoff commit (`handoff_commit`): the issue's implementer completed at that commit and GitHub lists no open pull request on the branch, so the issue stays in `implementing`. Tell the implementer (`envoy_publish` to its role topic) to open the pull request from that branch; the daemon records it and moves the issue to `testing` once it is open. When the work should not go on, decide as the `pr-closed-unmerged` row says. It comes once per handoff commit. |
| `review-stuck` | A review round the daemon cannot end on its own, in one of three ways: no review decides the round, the approval is of code the current head may not carry, or GitHub will not merge the head. The `review-stuck` paragraphs of your own role prompt give each one, its `topic` and what answers it. The third carries no `topic`. Its remedy, the close and the reopen at the same head, is for one named case only, and your role prompt gives the sort and the remedy's test in full; this row is their summary. Read what the base requires as `skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md` reads it, rulesets and branch protection both, where a `code_scanning` rule requires an analysis no status-check list names. Read what the head carries in two lists, every page: its check runs (counted by `gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/commits/<head>/check-runs --jq .total_count` and listed by `gh api --paginate 'repos/<owner>/<repo>/commits/<head>/check-runs?per_page=100' --jq '.check_runs[] | [.name, .status, .conclusion] | @tsv'`) and its commit statuses (`gh api --paginate 'repos/<owner>/<repo>/commits/<head>/status?per_page=100' --jq '.statuses[] | [.context, .state] | @tsv'`), since a commit status is no check run. A required item in either list is reported, and a check run whose status is `queued`, `requested`, `waiting`, `pending` or `in_progress`, or a commit status whose state is `pending`, is running, not missing, and a check run concluded `failure`, `timed_out`, `cancelled` or `startup_failure`, or a status in state `failure` or `error`, is red; never read the status endpoint's top-level `state`, which reads `pending` with no status at all. A required item in neither list falls in a named case: a fork pull request whose runs wait for a maintainer's approval (`action_required`), a check only a commit status would report, and a workflow another repository defines each take a person through a `dispatch ask`; a required workflow whose own path filter skips every file of the change gets no run, and a reopen sends the same event, so while the base still carries that filter the head takes a change to the workflow or to the head, and once the base has dropped it the head is the remedy's own case. The remedy's own case is a required workflow no event started on a pull request already open, and its test is the role prompt's: not a fork and no run `action_required`; a workflow file, as the merge commit holds it, has a job reporting the required item whose `on:` runs it for `pull_request` with no `types`, `branches` or path filter that excludes this change; and neither list holds a run of it. Such a head takes the reopen. A head that matches none of the named cases takes a person through a `dispatch ask`, never the reopen, since GitHub has more cases than these; so does a head whose required item still has no run once every run the reopen started has concluded. Close the pull request, wait a minute, and reopen it at the same head, since in cybermonklabs/legion a quicker reopen can have its CI run cancelled by the close's own run and leave `ci-ok` failing (`tests-closed.yaml`); tell the reviewer on its role topic to do it, since a repository action is the phase worker's. It costs two things: the close writes a `pr-closed-unmerged` notice, which is this close and ends no work, and the reopen starts the head's other checks again. It is for no other state: not a check that is running, not a check that is red, and not a head GitHub holds for a review the repository requires. A round whose head has no settled run gets no `review-stuck` notice of any kind, so read it in your own record with the `legion` tool's `read_record`, where the issue's `mergeWait` names the head: its `checkRuns` counts only the runs a CI settlement applied, so 0 there is no evidence of what GitHub holds, and you read the head's two lists before you act. A run still running there is waited for; for a GitHub Actions job GitHub ends the wait itself (24 hours queued on a self-hosted runner, `timeout-minutes` or 6 hours hosted and 5 days self-hosted running, 35 days for a whole workflow run), whose settlement moves the round, a `waiting` run waits on a person's approval of an environment, up to 30 days, and another App's check run, one a workflow created through the API and a `pending` commit status have no such bound and go to a person once they stand longer than their CI usually takes. Every run concluded while `checkRuns` stays 0 is a settlement not yet arrived, which Dispatch's CI reconcile pass repairs where it runs, a pass every 5 minutes reaching each head within a cycle of passes, so read the record again after a few passes; a head still at 0 then is sorted as above: a red required check is `request_backward_move` to `implementing`, a required item with no run takes the reopen only when the test matches, and every other head takes a person. A conflicting head waits in `mergeWait` only until two reads a minute apart confirm the conflict: an approval of a head with a confirmed conflict then ends the round, and once the issue enters `accepting` the daemon asks the implementer to merge the base forward and tells you with a `checks-red` notice. |
| `message-refused` | Dispatch refused a write the daemon was making on an issue of your tree, the way it would refuse every attempt, so the daemon makes it no more. A close reason is held to Dispatch's bound at the call that carries it (`close_root` answers `CLOSE_REASON_TOO_LONG`, naming the bound and how far over the text is), so this is a refusal that check did not predict. It names what the write carried (`refused`) and Dispatch's answer (`dispatch_answer`). On a status write the daemon writes the status all the same, so the status is set unless Dispatch refused the status itself, which `status_set` says — with the status set, only the reason beside it is on no issue unless `reason_posted` says it was posted, and you post what a person still needs yourself; with the status refused, the issue keeps the status it had while the daemon's own record reads the one the write carried (`status`), a disagreement no retry of the daemon's closes, so ask a person with `dispatch ask` to set it. On a message write nothing of what it carried reached the issue, and the notice gives its length (`length`) and its first line (`begins`), so post what a person needs yourself. |
| A reply on an ask you follow | Interpret the reply in the issue's design context. Answer in its thread (`dispatch comment --reply-to <comment id>`; under an open ask whose next move is yours, such as the approval request you must revise or hand back, `--reply-to-ask <ask id> --turn agent`, since a default-turn reply hands that request back to the human and a corrected `--summary` is then refused) where the reply carries a question, a disagreement, a correction or a fact you do not have, and whenever you are unsure; an approval, or an answer that settles the question with nothing left to say, draws no comment and no message back to the person who answered. Then adjust the plan or relay the reply via `envoy_publish` to the responsible worker's role token, which an answer needing nothing from you still needs; scope and product decisions remain with you. |
| `worker-died` | Its `role` and `phase`. That role's claim failed: its launches or prompts ran out, or its provider refused every turn of its session (its `failure.detail` reads `the provider refused <n> turns in a row: <message>`), or its system prompt is too large for its pod's start frame (a `launch_refused` with no count at its limit, whose `detail` names the role, the size and the limit: only a shorter prompt starts it, so a `retry` before the prompt is cut fails again at once; `escalate` it). For a phase worker the daemon holds the issue (phase `held`) and starts nothing more on it. A `worker-died` whose `role` is `architect` on a child is about that child's own architect, and it reaches you as the architect above it. Nothing is held, so there is nothing to `retry_or_escalate`. The child's notices come to you until that architect runs again, and the daemon starts it again, with fresh budgets, at the child's next phase start. A re-admission of your tree while the child stands in a phase, or an operator's retry, starts it again too. Meanwhile, act as section 2 says, and accept the child yourself if it reaches `accepting` (section 6). A `worker-died` whose `working` names a phase its `role` does not work holds nothing either. It carries `failure` as well (the notice of a held phase, and that of a role which does not work the issue's phase; the one that ends a closed issue's production-check look carries neither `failure` nor `phase`, and its `look` names the look that lost its role): `cause`, one cause word of the seven the daemon chooses from (`launch_refused`, `died`, `never_registered`, `stale_plugin`, `prompt_retires_exhausted`, `provider_error`, and `unrecorded` for a failure recorded before the daemon kept a cause), `detail`, the sentence written beside that word, `death` and `startFailure`, what the runtime and the launcher last said of this claim where it recorded either, and `launchFailures`, `deaths`, `promptFailures` and `promptRetires`, each a `count` beside the `limit` it ran out at. The claim's record in `read_record` carries the same `failure`, and the issue's `heldFrom` names the phase the hold took it out of. Reassess the work, then decide with `retry_or_escalate`: `retry` when the failure looks agent-specific or transient, `escalate` to hand the held issue to the controller when it looks environmental. `reason` is optional on either decision: a `retry`'s reaches the restarted worker's task, so say in it what you changed or what you want done differently, and an `escalate`'s reaches the controller as the `escalation` key of the `held` notice that records it, so say in it why the tree cannot go on without a decision of the controller's. A `retry` of a claim that failed on refusals starts a **new session**, since resuming the one the provider refused would be refused again: that worker has read none of its own earlier work, and is told so in its first task, which names its branch and the handoffs to read. Expect it to re-read before it continues, and tell it anything only you know. |
| `worker-stalled` | Its `role` and the record the daemon read. The daemon's stall backstop acted on that worker's stalled turn: its agent had not stepped for longer than the `worker_stall_backstop_hours` this deployment gives the role, and most often the daemon ended the turn and brought the same session back on a new process. The record names when the agent last stepped (`last_step`) and how long ago that was (`silent_for`), the bound it passed (`bound`), when the turn began (`turn_started`), and the last call the daemon saw, with its tool (`call_tool`), its call id (`call_id`), when it started (`call_started`) and whether it ended (`call_ended`) — and never a call's arguments, whose text the daemon keeps in no column. Its `end` says what the daemon did as well, which is not always a relaunch: where the daemon's wake pacing parked the replacement, the task was taken back and the launch that replaces the stalled process is queued, due at the moment `wake_due` names, and that process runs on until the launch's turn, which may start nothing — a suspension waiting for the claim, a refused lifecycle recheck or the tree's close can end the claim there instead; where a suspension was already waiting for that turn, that suspension ran instead of a replacement and the claim is suspended with nothing queued for it, waiting on a resume; where the launch that replaces the process could not be written, the daemon is still retrying that launch; and where every try at that launch was refused, the claim has failed, and the `worker-died` that comes with it is yours to answer as that row says. Its `stop` says what became of the stalled process too, for every end but the parked one: that it was stopped, that the daemon made no stop, or that the runtime did not confirm the stop, in which case that process may be running still and holding what it runs on — tell the controller of that one, because the daemon stops it again only at its next launch of that claim or at its next restart. No second notice follows any of these unless the daemon restarts while a relaunch is parked, which decides the claim again. The task that worker was serving is still owed to it, and what its return waits on is the one the notice names — the parked launch's turn, the retried launch, or a resume; the stall itself charged the claim nothing, `launch_failures` counts what the replacement charged, and no phase moved: on every end but the failed one the phase goes on by itself and you start nothing. Your part is to read what the record says the agent was doing and to judge whether that work needs a decision of yours — a call that hung on something the plan did not foresee is one, a turn that comes back and finishes is not. A stall of a root architect's own session reaches that architect the same way, on its own issue, and the controller is told of it too. A stall of a child's own architect reaches the architect above it. |
| `worker-silent` | Its `role`, its `phase` and the record the daemon read. That worker stopped between turns rather than inside one: its process is up, it holds no task, and its agent has not stepped for longer than the `worker_silence_minutes` this deployment sets, so nothing in the daemon and nothing in that process was going to prompt it again. The record names when the agent last stepped (`last_step`), how long ago that was (`silent_for`) and the bound (`bound`), and `sent` says what the daemon wrote about it, read from the phase entry the role's phase row names: the start row that sends the work of that entry, where the worker's claim was never given it, or the start row that sends the phase's task again, where the claim is on that entry already, naming the entry (`entry`) and the entry the claim was last given (`given`) where the row holds them. That row is written and the worker is coming back with that work: read the issue's record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` to see where the phase stands, and start nothing yourself. The one thing that still refuses the start is a suspension an operator holds over the claim, which the outbox reads when it runs the row: ask the operator to resume that claim, and message the role on its topic (`envoy_publish`) where its phase cannot wait. It comes once for each silence, and a worker that takes a step and then goes quiet again is told again. |
| `worker-background-stalled` | Its `role`, its `phase` and the record the daemon read. That worker's own session reports background work of its own — an async job, a subagent, a background task — still running, and it has reported the same thing without change for longer than the `worker_background_hours` this deployment sets. The record names the kinds the session named (`work`), when it last reported them (`reported_at`), how long ago that was (`stood_for`) and the bound (`bound`). Nothing was sent to that worker and nothing will be sent for this: the daemon cannot tell a job that hung from a long one, so it names the worker to you and decides nothing. Your part is to read what the session says it is waiting on and to judge whether that work needs a decision of its own — a job waiting on a surface that does not exist, or on a person, is one; a long build or a long test run is not. Read the issue's record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` for where the phase stands, then either leave the work to finish or message the role on its topic (`envoy_publish`) with what you decide. The worker is not prompted by the silence repair while it reports such work, so nothing else is coming for it either. It comes once for each standing report: a worker whose reported work changes — a job that ends and another that starts — is told of again under the new report. |
| `held` | Its `role` and `phase` (the phase the issue left), and `hold`, what held it. `hold: failure`: that phase's worker ran out of launches or prompts: the issue is held (phase `held`), the notice carries the same `failure` record as the `worker-died` that comes with it, which is yours to answer with `retry_or_escalate` as that row says, and the controller hears of the hold too. `hold: escalation`, with `reason: "escalated"`, records your own `escalate`: the controller has the issue now, it carries no `failure`, and you start nothing for it, so it is `low`: read it at your next turn. It carries `escalation` instead, your own `reason` from that `escalate`, and carries none where you named none: the controller reads it as your account of why the issue is its, beside the cause word in the claim's own `failure` record, so say in it what the tree cannot go on without. `hold: carried` is the hold of a root carried into your tree, told to you at admission: answer it with `retry_or_escalate` as for a failure. Your `retry` is not the only release: a `todo` written on a held root releases the hold the same way and at the same price of nothing, whoever writes it, so an issue you left held may be working that phase again when you next read it. |
| `session-lost` | Its `role`, its `phase` and the runtime's account of the failed resume (`detail`). That role lost the session it recorded: no session store holds it. The daemon runs the role in a fresh agent, which has read none of its own earlier turns, and only what it pushed and committed survives. Nothing is held, and you start nothing for it. Tell a phase worker that lost its session anything that only you know of its work (`envoy_publish` to its role topic). |

## A root that joins your tree, and a refused release

- A root that waited in the admission queue, never ran and never left another tree joins your tree once a person makes it your child, if it is `todo`, and otherwise at your release of it (the `child-status` row of [wake routing](skill://legion-architect/references/wake-routing.md)); once in, it is held for its blockers like any child you release. A root that left another tree, handed over by its finished tree or admitted as an orphan, stays its own tree and never joins yours, even one that was your root's child before your root was admitted again. A labelled `todo` issue under a child you have not released, unless it ran as a tree of its own or left another tree, is held under that unreleased child (not a blocked-by hold): it waits for no slot until your release of it or of that child brings it in, with any `todo` child of its own.
- A release that cannot take a child in is refused 409 and writes nothing, its message naming the child and why: release the rest again without it. `CHILD_NOT_ADOPTABLE` is an issue that runs or ran as a tree of its own, carries a run handed over to it, or was handed over (or admitted as an orphan) as a root of its own, and a member of a closed tree that holds an admission slot when you release it, which the daemon leaves where it stands: leave it there, or ask the controller. `CHILD_IN_OTHER_TREE` is another tree's child. One kind of other tree's child does join yours: a member of a tree whose root the daemon's record says is `done` and whose linger has run out, standing at `admitted` with no slot of its own, which your release records in your tree with its repository kept and its generation raised by one — at `admitted` when it is `todo`, and in any other status as a parked child at `done`, which a `todo` runs again. `admission.held` reads `tree_closed` for more issues than a release takes: a member whose tree's linger still runs, and a member past `admitted`, read that word too and stay refused (the `tree_closed` row of `skill://legion-controller/references/admission.md`). When the tree it names is a root held under an unreleased child of yours (a root that queued and never ran, so no architect has started on it), release that root, or the unreleased child it sits under, instead: its children come with it. Otherwise re-parent the child back in Dispatch, or ask that tree's architect or the controller.
- A child's own architect releases, parks and reruns only the issues under its own issue. `release_children`, `park_child` and `rerun_child` refuse any other issue of the tree with 403 `ISSUE_NOT_OWNED` and change nothing: the tree root, the architect's own issue, a sibling of it and an issue under a sibling are another architect's. Send such a move to the architect above you.
